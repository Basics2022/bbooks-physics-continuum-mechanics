11.1. Kirchhoff plate#

Following the procedure in Timoshenko beam then simplified to the Euler-Bernoulli beam model, this section contains:

  • Kinematic assumptions, and the elastic constitutive law

  • Internal actions

  • Balance equations

11.1.1. Kinematic assumptions#

Displacement.

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \mathbf{s}(x,y,z,t) & = \mathbf{s}_P(x,y,t) + \boldsymbol{\theta}(x,y,t) \times \mathbf{r}_P(x,y) = \\ & = \mathbf{s}_P(x,y,t) + \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( + z \theta_y(x,y) \right) - \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( z \theta_x(x,y) \right) \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

assuming negligible warping, and without in-plane roation (drilling d.o.f., \(\theta_z\)).

Strain.

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{zz} & = s_{Pz/z} \\ \varepsilon_{xx} & = s_{Px/x} + z \theta_{y/x} \\ \varepsilon_{yy} & = s_{Py/y} - z \theta_{x/y} \\ 2 \varepsilon_{zx} & = s_{Pz/x} + \theta_y \\ 2 \varepsilon_{zy} & = s_{Pz/y} - \theta_x \\ 2 \varepsilon_{xy} & = s_{Px/y} + s_{Py/x} + z \theta_{y/y} - z \theta_{x/x} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Kirchhoff assumptions imply \(\theta_x = s_{Pz/y}\), \(\theta_y = -s_{Pz/x}\), and thus no “transverse” shear stress \(\varepsilon_{zx} = \varepsilon_{zy} = 0\). Negligible volumetric strain in the direction perpendicular to the surface of the plate, \(\varepsilon_{zz} = 0\). Under these assumptions, only 3 components of the strain tensor can be different from zero: \(\varepsilon_{xx}\), \(\varepsilon_{yy}\), \(\varepsilon_{xy}\).

11.1.2. Internal actions#

Cutting an elementary surface with unit-normal vector pointing outwards, the force acting on it is

\[\mathbf{F}_{\hat{\mathbf{n}}} := \int_{A} \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} \ , \]

so that

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \mathbf{F}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} & = \int_{A_x} \hat{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} = \int_{A_x} \left\{ \hat{\mathbf{x}} \sigma_{xx} + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \sigma_{xy} + \hat{\mathbf{z}} \sigma_{xz} \right\} \\ \mathbf{F}_{\hat{\mathbf{y}}} & = \int_{A_x} \hat{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} = \int_{A_y} \left\{ \hat{\mathbf{x}} \sigma_{yx} + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \sigma_{yy} + \hat{\mathbf{z}} \sigma_{yz} \right\} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The internal moment, with \(\mathbf{r} = z \hat{\mathbf{z}}\) reads

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \mathbf{M}_{\hat{\mathbf{n}}} & := \int_{A} \mathbf{r} \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

so that

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \mathbf{M}_{\hat{\mathbf{x}}} & := \int_{A_x} \mathbf{r} \times ( \hat{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) = \int_{A_x} \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( -z \sigma_{xy} \right) + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( z \sigma_{xx} \right) \\ \mathbf{M}_{\hat{\mathbf{y}}} & := \int_{A_y} \mathbf{r} \times ( \hat{\mathbf{y}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) = \int_{A_y} \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( -z \sigma_{yy} \right) + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( z \sigma_{yx} \right) \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Remark \(M_{xx} = - M_{yy}\).

11.1.3. Balance equations#

Balance equations for a beam can be obtained integrating indefinite balance equations for a 3-dimensional solid on the sections \(A(z)\) of the beam, with some further assumption on non-rigid contributions to displacement.

Momentum equation. Using a “Cartesian” elementary volume with sides \(\Delta x\), \(\Delta y\), \(h\)

\[\mathbf{\rho}_0 \ddot{\mathbf{s}} = \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \mathbf{f}\]

gives

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = - m \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{S}_{P} \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol\theta} + \mathbf{F}_{x/x} + \mathbf{F}_{y/y} + \mathbf{f} = \\ & = - m \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{S}_{P} \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol\theta} + \nabla_{(2)} \cdot \mathbb{F} + \mathbf{f} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Details
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \mathbf{0} & = - \int_{\Delta V} \rho_0 \ddot{\mathbf{s}} + \int_{\Delta V} \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \int_{\Delta V} \rho_0 \mathbf{g} = \\ & = - \Delta x \Delta y \, \int_{h} \rho_0 \left( \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{r}_P \times \ddot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \right) + \\ & \quad + \int_{A_x(x)} \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \int_{A_x(x+\Delta x)} \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \\ & \quad + \int_{A_y(y)} \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \int_{A_y(y+\Delta y)} \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \\ & \quad + \int_{\Delta A_{lat,sup}} \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \int_{\Delta A_{lat,inf}} \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \\ & \quad + \Delta A \, \int_{h} \rho_0 \mathbf{g} = \\ & = - \Delta x \Delta y \, \int_{h} \rho_0 \left( \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{r}_P \times \ddot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \right) + \\ & \quad + \Delta x \, \partial_x \int_{A_x(x)} \hat{\mathbf{x}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \\ & \quad + \Delta y \, \partial_y \int_{A_y(y)} \hat{\mathbf{y}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \\ & \quad + \int_{\Delta A_{lat,sup-inf}} \mathbf{f}^{lat} + \Delta A \, \int_{h} \rho_0 \mathbf{g} = \\ & = \Delta A \left[ - m \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{S}_{P} \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol\theta} + \mathbf{F}_{x/x} + \mathbf{F}_{y/y} + \mathbf{f} \right] \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Angular momentum equation.

\[\mathbf{r}_P \times \mathbf{\rho}_0 \ddot{\mathbf{s}} = \mathbf{r}_P \times \left( \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \mathbf{f} \right)\]

gives

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = - \mathbf{S}_P^T \cdot \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{I}_{P} \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol\theta} + \mathbf{M}_{x/x} + \mathbf{M}_{y/y} - \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \mathbf{F} + \mathbf{m} = \\ & = - \mathbf{S}_P^T \cdot \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{I}_{P} \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol\theta} + \nabla_{(2)} \cdot \mathbb{M} - \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \left( \mathbb{F} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{z}} \right) + \mathbf{m} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Details
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \mathbf{0} & = - \int_{\Delta V} \rho_0 \mathbf{r}_P \times \ddot{\mathbf{s}} + \int_{\Delta V} \mathbf{r}_P \times \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma + \int_{\Delta V} \rho_0 \mathbf{r}_P \times \mathbf{g} = \\ & = - \Delta A \, \int_{h} \rho_0 \mathbf{r}_P \times \left( \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{r}_P \times \ddot{\boldsymbol{\theta}} \right) + \dots \\ & = \Delta A \left[ - \mathbf{S}_P^T \cdot \ddot{\mathbf{s}}_P - \mathbf{I}_{P} \cdot \ddot{\boldsymbol\theta} + \mathbf{M}_{x/x} + \mathbf{M}_{y/y} + \mathbf{m}^{lat} + \mathbf{m}_V \right] \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
Contribution of stress to moment

With \(\mathbf{r} = z \hat{\mathbf{z}}\), i.e. \(\mathbf{r} = z \, \delta_{i3} \, \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i = r_3 \, \delta_{i3} \, \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \),

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \int_{V} \mathbf{r} \times \nabla \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma & = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{V} \varepsilon_{ijk} r_j \sigma_{lk/l} = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{V} \varepsilon_{ijk} \left[ \left( r_j \sigma_{lk} \right)_{/l} - r_{j/l} \sigma_{lk} \right] = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{\partial V} \varepsilon_{ijk} r_j n_l \sigma_{lk} - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{V} \varepsilon_{ijk} r_{j/l} \sigma_{lk} = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{\partial V} \varepsilon_{ijk} r_j n_l \sigma_{lk} - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{V} \varepsilon_{ijk} \left( r_3 \delta_{j3} \right)_{/l} \sigma_{lk} = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{\partial V} \varepsilon_{ijk} r_j n_l \sigma_{lk} - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{V} \varepsilon_{ijk} \delta_{l3} \delta_{j3} \sigma_{lk} = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{\partial V} \varepsilon_{ijk} r_j n_l \sigma_{lk} - \hat{\mathbf{e}}_i \int_{V} \varepsilon_{i3k} \sigma_{3k} = \\ & = \int_{\partial V} \mathbf{r}_P \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) - \hat{\mathbf{x}} \int_{V} \varepsilon_{132} \sigma_{32} - \hat{\mathbf{y}} \int_{V} \varepsilon_{231} \sigma_{31} = \\ & = \int_{\partial V} \mathbf{r}_P \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) + \hat{\mathbf{x}} \int_{V} \sigma_{32} - \hat{\mathbf{y}} \int_{V} \sigma_{31} = \\ & = \int_{\partial V} \mathbf{r}_P \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) + \hat{\mathbf{x}} F_{zy} - \hat{\mathbf{y}} F_{zx} = \\ & = \int_{\partial V} \mathbf{r}_P \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) - \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \mathbf{F}_z \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

For an elementary plate element \(\Delta A\), the first contribution contains internal moments on 4 surfaces cutting the plate and the contribution of the pair of lateral surfaces, that can be summed with the volume contribution to get load from linear density loads,

\[\int_{\partial \Delta V} \mathbf{r}_P \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) = \Delta y \, \Delta x \, \mathbf{M}_{x/x} + \Delta x \, \Delta y \, \mathbf{M}_{y/y} + \int_{A_{lat,sup}} \mathbf{r}_P \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) + \int_{A_{lat,inf}} \mathbf{r}_P \times ( \hat{\mathbf{n}} \cdot \boldsymbol\sigma ) \]

The contribution of the lateral surfaces becom

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} & \int_{A_{lat,sup}} z^{sup} \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \mathbf{f}^{sup} + \int_{A_{lat,inf}} z^{inf} \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \mathbf{f}^{inf} = \\ & \quad = \Delta x \, \Delta y \, \mathbf{m}^{lat} \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

with the definition \(\mathbf{m}^{lat} := z^{sup} \mathbf{f}^{sup} + z^{inf} \mathbf{f}^{inf}\). As an example, if the reference surface is the mid-surface, then \(z_{sup} = - z_{inf} = \tfrac{h}{2}\), and \(\mathbf{m}^{lat} = \tfrac{h}{2} \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \left( \mathbf{f}^{sup} - \mathbf{f}^{inf} \right)\).

For an inertially decoupled element, using Cartesian coordinates

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = - m \ddot{s}_{Px} + F_{xx/x} + F_{yx/y} + f_x \\ 0 & = - m \ddot{s}_{Py} + F_{xy/x} + F_{yy/y} + f_y \\ 0 & = - m \ddot{s}_{Pz} + F_{xz/x} + F_{yz/y} + f_z \\ 0 & = - I_x \ddot{\theta}_x + M_{xx/x} + M_{yx/y} + F_{zx} + m_x \\ 0 & = - I_y \ddot{\theta}_y + M_{xy/x} + M_{yy/y} - F_{zy} + m_y \\ 0 & = - I_z \ddot{\theta}_z + M_{xz/z} + M_{yz/y} + m_z \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Details of the simplified equations
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = F_{xx/x} + F_{yx/y} \\ 0 & = F_{xy/x} + F_{yy/y} \\ 0 & = F_{xz/x} + F_{yz/y} + f_z \\ 0 & = M_{xx/x} + M_{yx/y} + F_{zx} \\ 0 & = M_{xy/x} + M_{yy/y} - F_{zy} \\ 0 & = M_{xz/z} + M_{yz/y} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The vertical equilibrium becomes

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = f_z + F_{yz/y} + F_{xz/x} = \\ & = f_z - \left( M_{xx/x} + M_{yx/y} \right)_{/y} + \left( M_{xy/x} + M_{yy/y} \right)_{/x} \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Let the constitutive law be

\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xx} \\ \sigma_{yy} \\ \sigma_{xy} \end{bmatrix} = d \begin{bmatrix} 1 & m & 0 \\ m & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1-m \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{xx} \\ \varepsilon_{yy} \\ \varepsilon_{xy} \end{bmatrix} \ .\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{xx} & = s_{Px/x} + z \theta_{y/x} && = \dots = - z w_{/xx} \\ \varepsilon_{yy} & = s_{Py/y} - z \theta_{x/y} && = \dots = - z w_{/yy} \\ 2 \varepsilon_{xy} & = s_{Px/y} + s_{Py/x} + z \theta_{y/y} - z \theta_{x/x} && = \dots = - z ( w_{/xy} + w_{/yx}) = - z \, 2 w_{/xy} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{xx} & = - \int_{h} z \sigma_{xy} = D \, ( 1 - m ) w_{/xy} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{xy} & = \int_{h} z \sigma_{xx} = - D \, ( w_{/xx} + m \, w_{/yy} ) \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{yy} & = \int_{h} z \sigma_{xy} = - D \, ( 1 - m ) w_{/xy} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{yx} & = - \int_{h} z \sigma_{yy} = D \, ( w_{/yy} + m \, w_{/xx} ) \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The equation becomes

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} f_z & = M_{xx/xy} + M_{yx/yy} - M_{xy/xx} - M_{yy/yx} = \\ & = D \left\{ (1-m) w_{/xxyy} + w_{/yyyy} + m w_{/xxyy} + w_{/xxxx} + m w_{/xxyy} + ( 1 - m ) w_{/xxyy} \right\} = \\ & = D \left\{ w_{/xxxx} + 2 w_{/xxyy} + w_{/yyyy} \right\} = \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

i.e.

\[D \Delta^2 w = f_z \ .\]
