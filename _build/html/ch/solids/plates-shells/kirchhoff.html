Details of the simplified equations
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = F_{xx/x} + F_{yx/y} \\ 0 & = F_{xy/x} + F_{yy/y} \\ 0 & = F_{xz/x} + F_{yz/y} + f_z \\ 0 & = M_{xx/x} + M_{yx/y} + F_{yz} \\ 0 & = M_{xy/x} + M_{yy/y} - F_{xz} \\ 0 & = M_{xz/z} + M_{yz/y} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The vertical equilibrium becomes

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} 0 & = f_z + F_{yz/y} + F_{xz/x} = \\ & = f_z - \left( M_{xx/x} + M_{yx/y} \right)_{/y} + \left( M_{xy/x} + M_{yy/y} \right)_{/x} \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Let the constitutive law be

\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} \sigma_{xx} \\ \sigma_{yy} \\ \sigma_{xy} \end{bmatrix} = d \begin{bmatrix} 1 & m & 0 \\ m & 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 1-m \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \varepsilon_{xx} \\ \varepsilon_{yy} \\ \varepsilon_{xy} \end{bmatrix} \ .\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \varepsilon_{xx} & = s_{Px/x} + z \theta_{y/x} && = \dots = - z w_{/xx} \\ \varepsilon_{yy} & = s_{Py/y} - z \theta_{x/y} && = \dots = - z w_{/yy} \\ 2 \varepsilon_{xy} & = s_{Px/y} + s_{Py/x} + z \theta_{y/y} - z \theta_{x/x} && = \dots = - z ( w_{/xy} + w_{/yx}) = - z \, 2 w_{/xy} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{xx} & = - \int_{h} z \sigma_{xy} = D \, ( 1 - m ) w_{/xy} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{xy} & = \int_{h} z \sigma_{xx} = - D \, ( w_{/xx} + m \, w_{/yy} ) \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{yy} & = \int_{h} z \sigma_{xy} = - D \, ( 1 - m ) w_{/xy} \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} M_{yx} & = - \int_{h} z \sigma_{yy} = D \, ( w_{/yy} + m \, w_{/xx} ) \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

The equation becomes

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} f_z & = M_{xx/xy} + M_{yx/yy} - M_{xy/xx} - M_{yy/yx} = \\ & = D \left\{ (1-m) w_{/xxyy} + w_{/yyyy} + m w_{/xxyy} + w_{/xxxx} + m w_{/xxyy} + ( 1 - m ) w_{/xxyy} \right\} = \\ & = D \left\{ w_{/xxxx} + 2 w_{/xxyy} + w_{/yyyy} \right\} = \\ \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

i.e.

\[D \Delta^2 w = f_z \ .\]

11.2.1.1. Analytical solution of a rectangular plate#

Using Cartesian coordinates \((x,y)\), the domain of a rectangular plate reads \((x,y) = [0,L_x] \times [0,L_y]\).

11.2.1.1.1. Hinged boundary#

BOundary conditions. On the sides with \(x = 0\), \(x = L_x\), with free rotation around the side (zero component of the moment, as there’s no net reaction moment)

\[w(x_b, y ) = 0 \quad , \quad M_{xy}(x_b,y ) = 0 \ .\]

On the sides with \(y = 0\), \(y = L_y\), with free rotation around the side (zero component of the moment, as there’s no net reaction moment)

\[w(x , y_b) = 0 \quad , \quad M_{yx}(x ,y_b) = 0 \ .\]

Base functions. The solution can be found as a linear combination of the base functions satisfying the homogeneous essential boundary conditions,

\[\phi_{n_x n_y}(x,y) = \sin\left( n_x \frac{\pi x}{L_x} \right) \, \sin\left( n_y \frac{\pi y}{L_y} \right) \ ,\]

i.e. as

\[w(x,y) = \sum_{n_x, n_y \in \mathbb{N}} a_{n_x, n_y} \phi_{x_n, n_y}(x,y) \ .\]

Remark. This choice of base functions satisfies the essential boundary conditions about displacement, and identically satisfies also the natural boundary condition about moment, by setting equal to zero both individual contributions \(w_{/xx}\), \(w_{/yy}\) appearing in \(M_{xy} = - D(w_{/xx} + m w_{/yy})\), and \(M_{yx} = D(w_{/yy} + m w_{/xx})\).

Is this ok? Is this choice forcing extra constraints?

Double Laplacian. The double Laplacian of the base function \(\phi_{n_x n_y}(x,y)\) reads

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \Delta^2 \phi_{n_x n_y}(x,y) & = \phi_{n_x n_y/xxxx} + 2 \phi_{n_x n_y/xxyy} + \phi_{n_x n_y/yyyy} = \\ & = \left\{ \left( \frac{\pi n_x}{L_x} \right)^4 + 2 \left( \frac{\pi n_x}{L_x} \right)^2 \left( \frac{\pi n_y}{L_y} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{\pi n_y}{L_y} \right)^4 \right\} \phi_{n_x n_y}(x,y) \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Series exapnsion of external distributed load, \(f\), and solution by coefficient matching. Let the Fourier expansion of the load \(f(x,y)\) be

\[f(x,y) = \sum_{n_x,n_y \in \mathbb{N}} f_{n_x, n_y} \sin\left( \frac{n_x \pi x}{L_x} \right) \, \sin\left( \frac{n_y \pi y}{L_y} \right) = \sum_{n_x,n_y \in \mathbb{N}} f_{n_x, n_y} \phi_{n_x n_y}(x,y) \ ,\]

with

(11.1)#\[f_{n_x n_y} = \frac{4}{L_x L_y} \int_{x=0}^{L_x} \int_{y=0}^{L_y} f(x,y) \sin\left( \frac{n_x \pi x}{L_x} \right) \, \sin\left( \frac{n_y \pi y}{L_y} \right) \,dx \, dy \]

Then the solution of the problem is obtained by coefficient matching as

\[a_{n_x n_y} = \frac{1}{ \pi^4 D \left[ \left(\frac{n_x}{L_x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{n_y}{L_y}\right)^2 \right]^2} f_{n_x n_y} \ .\]

  • “Harmonic” load: let the load be \(f(x,y) = f_{11} \sin\left( \tfrac{\pi x}{L_x} \right) \sin \left( \tfrac{\pi y}{L_y} \right)\), then the solution can be represented by one base function only, i.e. \(\phi_{11}(x,y)\), while the coefficient of the linear expansion of any other base function is zero by coefficient matching. The coefficent \(a_{11}\) reads

    \[f_{11} = D \pi^4 \left\{ \left(\frac{1}{L_x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{L_y}\right)^2 \right\}^2 \, a_{11} \ ,\]

    i.e.

    \[w(x,y) = \frac{f_{11}}{D \pi^4} \left[ \left(\frac{1}{L_x}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{1}{L_y}\right)^2 \right]^{-1} \sin\left( \frac{n_x \pi x}{L_x} \right) \, \sin\left( \frac{n_y \pi y}{L_y} \right) \ .\]

  • Uniform load, \(f(x,y) = f\): only contributions with odd indices survive in the integrals (11.1), as

    \[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \frac{f_{n_x n_y}}{f} & = \frac{4}{L_x L_y} \int_{x=0}^{L_x} \int_{y=0}^{L_y} \sin\left( \frac{n_x \pi x}{L_x} \right) \, \sin\left( \frac{n_y \pi y}{L_y} \right) \,dx \, dy = \\ & = \frac{4}{L_x L_y} \frac{L_x}{n_x \pi} \frac{L_y}{n_y \pi} \left.\cos\left( \frac{n_x \pi x}{L_x} \right)\right|_{0}^{L_x} \left.\cos\left( \frac{n_y \pi y}{L_y} \right)\right|_{0}^{L_y} = \\ & = \left\{ \begin{aligned} n_k = 2 m_k + 1: & \quad \frac{16}{\pi^2 (2m_x+1)(2m_y+1)} \\ n_k = 2 m_k : & \quad 0 \\ \end{aligned} \right. \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

11.2.1.2. Discussion of the meaning of the space derivatives of the displacement field#

Detailed discussion of the meaning of the space derivatives of the displacement field \(\ w(x,y)\).

Using here 2-dimensional nabla differential operator

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} f_z & = D \Delta^2 w = \\ & = D \Delta \, \Delta w = \\ & = D ( \nabla \cdot \nabla ) ( \nabla \cdot \nabla ) w \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

Gradient of the out-of-plane displacement field. It’s immediately related to the field of infinitiesimal rotations,

\[\nabla w(x,y) = \hat{\mathbf{x}} w_{/x} + \hat{\mathbf{y}} w_{/y} = - \hat{\mathbf{x}} \theta_{y} + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \theta_{x} = \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \boldsymbol\theta(x,y) \ .\]

with \(\boldsymbol\theta = ( \theta_x, \theta_y, 0)\). Exploiting the vector identity \(\mathbf{a} \times ( \mathbf{b} \times \mathbf{c} ) = ( \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{c} ) \mathbf{b} - ( \mathbf{a} \cdot \mathbf{b}) \mathbf{c}\), and the orthogonality between \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\) and \(\boldsymbol\theta\), \(\hat{\mathbf{z}} \cdot \boldsymbol\theta = 0\), then

\[\boldsymbol\theta = - \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \nabla w \ .\]

Since \(w_{/xy} = w_{/yx}\), then \(-\theta_{y/y} = \theta_{x/x}\), i.e. the field of the infinitesimal rotation is solenoidal,

\[\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol\theta = 0 \ .\]

Divergence of the gradient of \(w(x,y)\).

\[\Delta w = \nabla \cdot \nabla w = w_{/xx} + w_{/yy} = - \theta_{y/x} + \theta_{x/y} = - \hat{\mathbf{z}} \cdot \nabla \times \boldsymbol \theta \ .\]

Gradient of the divergence of the gradient of \(w(x,y)\).

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \nabla \Delta w & = \nabla \nabla \cdot \nabla w = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( w_{/xxx} + w_{/yyx} \right) + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( w_{/xxy} + w_{/yyy} \right) = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( - \theta_{y/xx} + \theta_{x/yx} \right) + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( - \theta_{y/xy} + \theta_{x/yy} \right) = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( - \theta_{y/xx} - \theta_{y/yy} \right) + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( \theta_{x/xx} + \theta_{x/yy} \right) = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{x}} \left( - \Delta \theta_y \right) + \hat{\mathbf{y}} \left( \Delta \theta_x \right) = \\ & = \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \Delta \boldsymbol\theta \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

and the inverse relation, exploiting orthogonality of \(\hat{\mathbf{z}}\) and \(\Delta \boldsymbol\theta\),

\[\Delta \boldsymbol\theta = - \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \nabla \Delta w \ .\]

Comparing with \(\Delta \boldsymbol\theta = D^{-1} \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \mathbf{F}_z\), it immediately follows that

\[\mathbf{F}_z = - D \nabla \Delta w \ ,\]

compatible with the two expressions of the equilibrium equation, \(f_z = D \nabla \cdot \nabla \Delta w\), or \(f_z = - \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}\).

\[\begin{split}\left\{ \begin{aligned} M_{xx} & = D( 1 - m ) w_{/xy} \\ M_{xy} & = - D( w_{/xx} + m w_{/yy} ) \\ M_{yx} & = D( w_{/yy} + m w_{/xx} ) \\ M_{yy} & = - D( 1 - m ) w_{/xy} \\ \end{aligned} \right.\end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \begin{bmatrix} M_{xx} & M_{yx} \\ M_{xy} & M_{yy} \end{bmatrix} & = D \begin{bmatrix} w_{/xy} & w_{/yy} \\ - w_{/xx} & - w_{/xy} \end{bmatrix} + m D \begin{bmatrix} -w_{/xy} & w_{/xx} \\ -w_{/yy} & w_{/xy} \end{bmatrix} = \\ & = D \begin{bmatrix} \theta_{x/x} & \theta_{x/y} \\ \theta_{y/x} & \theta_{y/y} \end{bmatrix} + m D \begin{bmatrix} -\theta_{x/x} & -\theta_{y/x} \\ -\theta_{x/y}& -\theta_{y/y} \end{bmatrix} \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
\[\mathbb{M} = D \nabla \boldsymbol\theta - m D \nabla^T \boldsymbol\theta \ .\]

The Laplacian of the displacement field \(\Delta w\) reads

\[\Delta w = w_{/xx} + w_{/yy} = \frac{M_{yx} - M_{xy}}{(1+m) D}\]

The divergence of \(\mathbb{M}\) reads

\[\nabla \cdot \mathbb{M} = \partial_{i} M_{ij} = \partial_{i} \left( D \theta_{j/i} - m D \theta_{i/j} \right) = D \theta_{j/ii} - m D \theta_{i/ij} = D \theta_{j/ii} = D \Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} \ . \]

having exploited the relation \(\nabla \cdot \boldsymbol\theta = 0\) to set \(\theta_{i/ij} = 0\).

\[\begin{split} \begin{bmatrix} \theta_{x/x} & \theta_{x/y} \\ \theta_{y/x} & \theta_{y/y} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} w_{/xy} & w_{/yy} \\ - w_{/xx} & - w_{/xy} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cdot & 1 \\ - 1 & \cdot \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} w_{/xx} & w_{/xy} \\ w_{/yx} & w_{ /yy} \end{bmatrix} \end{split}\]
\[\begin{split} \begin{bmatrix} \theta_{x/x} & \theta_{x/y} \\ \theta_{y/x} & \theta_{y/y} \end{bmatrix}^T = \begin{bmatrix} w_{/xy} & w_{/yy} \\ - w_{/xx} & - w_{/xy} \end{bmatrix}^T = \begin{bmatrix} w_{/xx} & w_{/xy} \\ w_{/yx} & w_{ /yy} \end{bmatrix}^T \begin{bmatrix} \cdot & -1 \\ 1 & \cdot \end{bmatrix} \end{split}\]

Angular momentum equilibrium equation reads

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} \mathbf{0} & = \nabla_{(2)} \cdot \mathbb{M} - \hat{\mathbf{z}}\times \left( \mathbb{F} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{z}} \right) = \\ & = \nabla_{(2)} \cdot \mathbb{M} - \hat{\mathbf{z}}\times \mathbf{F}_z \ , \end{aligned}\end{split}\]

or

\[\begin{split}\mathbf{0} = \begin{bmatrix} F_{yz} \\ -F_{xz} \end{bmatrix} + \begin{bmatrix} M_{xx/x} + M_{yx/y} \\ M_{xy/x} + M_{yy/y} \end{bmatrix} \end{split}\]
\[\begin{split}\begin{bmatrix} F_{xz} \\ F_{yz} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} M_{xy/x} + M_{yy/y} \\ - M_{xx/x} - M_{yx/y} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} \cdot & 1 \\ -1 & \cdot \end{bmatrix} \nabla \cdot \mathbb{M} = D \begin{bmatrix} \cdot & 1 \\ -1 & \cdot \end{bmatrix} \Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} \ ,\end{split}\]

and thus,

\[\begin{split}\Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} = D^{-1} \begin{bmatrix} \cdot & -1 \\ 1 & \cdot \end{bmatrix} \mathbf{F}_z = D^{-1} \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \mathbf{F}_z \ .\end{split}\]

Recalling the relation \(\theta_x = w_{/y}\), \(\theta_y = - w_{/x}\), then

\[\begin{split}\mathbf{F}_z = D \begin{bmatrix} \cdot & 1 \\ -1 & \cdot \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} \Delta w_{/y} \\ - \Delta w_{/x} \end{bmatrix} = - D \begin{bmatrix} \Delta w_{/x} \\ \Delta w_{/y} \end{bmatrix} = - D \nabla \Delta w \ .\end{split}\]

Comparing with \(\nabla \nabla \cdot \nabla w = \hat{\mathbf{z}} \times \Delta \boldsymbol\theta\),

\[\begin{split}\begin{aligned} f_z & = - \nabla \cdot \mathbf{F}_z = \\ & = - \nabla \cdot \left( D \, \begin{bmatrix} \cdot & -1 \\ 1 & \cdot \end{bmatrix} \Delta \boldsymbol{\theta} \right) = \\ & = D \Delta^2 w \ . \end{aligned}\end{split}\]
